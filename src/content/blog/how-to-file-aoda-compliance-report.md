**The short answer:** if your Ontario business or non-profit has 20 or more employees, you have to file an accessibility compliance report through the government's [Accessibility Compliance Reporting Portal](https://accessibilityreporting.ontario.ca/) by **December 31, 2026**. It's an online form of yes/no questions. Most businesses finish it in one sitting once they have the right details on hand.

Here's what you need, how to file it, and the one question that trips up businesses with a website.

## Who has to file, and when

| Organization | Files a report? | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Businesses and non-profits, 20+ employees in Ontario | Yes, every 3 years | December 31, 2026 |
| Businesses and non-profits, 1–19 employees | No (other AODA rules still apply) | — |
| Designated public sector (municipalities, etc.) | Yes, every 2 years | December 31, 2025 (passed) |

Source: [Ontario, "Completing your accessibility compliance report"](https://www.ontario.ca/page/completing-your-accessibility-compliance-report).

The report is a self-assessment. You're confirming that you meet the AODA requirements that currently apply to an organization of your size, things like accessible customer service, staff training, written policies, and (at 50+ employees) your website.

## What you need before you start

Have these ready so you don't stall halfway through:

- Your organization's **legal name**
- Your **business number (BN9)**, the first nine digits on your federal or provincial tax return
- Your **number of employees** in Ontario
- The name and contact details of your **certifier**: a senior officer with the legal authority to say the report is complete and accurate

## How to file it, step by step

1. Go to the [Accessibility Compliance Reporting Portal](https://accessibilityreporting.ontario.ca/) and select **Sign In/Create Account**. Register with an email address and password.
2. Sign in, select **New Submission**, and choose **Accessibility Compliance Report**.
3. Answer the questions. They're tailored to your organization type and employee count, and each one links to an explanation of the requirement behind it.
4. Select **Submit** at the bottom of the form.

You'll get a confirmation email with a confirmation number, sent to both your certifier and your primary contact. Keep it. If you get stuck, the ministry's AODA line is 1-866-515-2025, or email AODA.assistance@ontario.ca.

## The website question (50+ employees)

If you have 50 or more employees, the report asks about your website. In Ontario's [official guide to the report](https://files.ontario.ca/msaa-bnp-learn-how-to-complete-your-accessibility-compliance-report-en-2023-07-07.pdf), the question reads:

> Do all your organization's internet websites conform to World Wide Web Consortium Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.0 Level AA (except for live captions and pre-recorded audio descriptions)?

It also asks you to list the names and addresses of all your public web content, **including websites, social media pages, and apps**.

This is where most businesses guess. A yes is a claim, signed off by a senior officer, that every page meets an international standard most owners have never read. If nobody has actually checked the site, you don't know the answer.

If you have 20 to 49 employees, the website standard doesn't apply to you yet, but the same barriers still turn away customers who can't book, call, or buy.

## If your honest answer is no

Answer no. Ontario's guide is clear that a report showing you're **not fully compliant doesn't trigger automatic enforcement**. The ministry offers tools and support and agrees on a schedule for when you'll report full compliance.

The real risk is answering yes when it isn't true, or not filing at all. Enforcement can include orders to comply and financial penalties, and a corporation convicted of an offence under the Act can be fined up to $100,000 a day.

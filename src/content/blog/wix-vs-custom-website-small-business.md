Wix is everywhere. It's cheap, the ads make it look easy, and you can technically have something live in an afternoon. For a lot of small businesses, Wix (or Squarespace, or a ready-made WordPress theme) is the default starting point.

But "good enough to launch" and "good enough to grow" are different things. Here's an honest look at what you're actually choosing between, where Wix and templates work, where they fall short, and how to know which situation you're in.

## What a template actually is

Wix, Squarespace, and pre-built WordPress themes all work the same way: you start from a layout designed to work for any business in any industry. Your logo, colours, text, and photos go in, and you end up with something that looks like your business on the surface.

The underlying structure, the page flow, the visual hierarchy, was designed for a hypothetical generic business, not yours. You're adapting your business to fit a mould instead of the other way around.

A custom site starts from the opposite end: your customers. Who they are, what they're trying to do when they land on your page, and what question they need answered before they'll pick up the phone. The layout follows from that, and nothing is there just because it came with the template.

## Where Wix genuinely works

If you need a simple online presence, a few pages, basic contact info, nothing complex, Wix can do that. It's faster to get started than hiring someone, and the templates have improved a lot over the years.

For a brand new business that needs to exist online while they figure out their market, Wix is fine. It's not a long-term solution, but it gets you there.

## Where Wix starts to hurt you

### Speed on mobile

Wix sites are notoriously slow on mobile. The platform loads a lot of its own infrastructure on every page: editor tooling, app scripts, font loaders, code your visitors don't need but download anyway, and you don't control most of it. On a slow phone connection, this adds seconds to your load time, and slow pages lose customers. ([Here's how to make a slow site faster](/blog/how-to-make-your-website-faster), and why Wix and WordPress are hard to fix.)

A typical Wix site scores 40–60 on Google's mobile speed test. A custom-built site should be 90+. That gap costs money: Akamai's study of online retailers found that [even a 100-millisecond delay in load time can cut conversion rates by 7%](https://www.bandt.com.au/study-even-100-millisecond-delays-can-impact-performance-retail-websites/).

### You look like everyone else

Wix has thousands of users in every city. Templates are recognizable, not always to your customers consciously, but there's a sameness to them. Your competitor in Oshawa might be running the same template with different colours. In a market like Toronto, with hundreds of businesses in any category, looking identical to your competitors is a real disadvantage.

### Small changes get harder over time

Template platforms are easy to edit, until you want something the template wasn't built for: a different booking flow, a new kind of page, a section that doesn't exist yet. Then every change becomes a workaround, workarounds pile up, and small edits take longer than they should. With a custom site there's no template to fight. It's just code, and it can do what you need.

### You don't actually own it

With Wix, your site lives on their platform. If they change their pricing, change their terms, or go under, you're scrambling. You can't export your site and take it somewhere else cleanly. You're a tenant, not an owner.

A custom-built site: you own the code, the content, the domain. Everything is yours. If you ever want to move, you can.

### No real analytics setup

Wix has basic built-in stats, but they don't tell you much. You can see page views, but you can't easily see where your traffic is coming from, which pages are turning visitors into leads, or where people are dropping off.

Without that data, you're guessing what's working, and that's expensive guessing.

## Which one is right for you?

**A template (Wix, Squarespace, a WordPress theme)** makes sense if you're just starting out, testing a business idea, or need something live quickly on a minimal budget.

**A custom site** makes sense if you've been in business for a while, you're spending money on marketing, you care about showing up on Google, and you want a site that actually represents your business. (Here are [the real benefits of a custom website](/blog/benefits-of-a-custom-website-small-business), and when it's *not* worth it.)

You've outgrown Wix or your template, and it's time for a [website redesign](/website-redesign-toronto), when:
- Your site is slow and you're losing customers to it
- You're spending money on ads or SEO but can't tell if it's working
- You want to look more professional than your current site allows
- You're tired of paying Wix monthly and still needing to pay someone every time something needs to change

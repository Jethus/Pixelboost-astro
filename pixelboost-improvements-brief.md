# Pixelboost site — improvement brief for Claude Code

Context: Pixelboost (pixelboost.ca) is a solo web design/dev studio targeting Canadian SMBs in the Toronto / GTA / Durham Region area. Astro one-pager + Blog + Blog post + About + Contact. Brand: cream / mint / near-black with a green accent, rounded cards, strong type hierarchy, warm and direct "just me, no agency" voice. Design and content are already strong — the goal here is squeezing more traffic and conversions out of what exists, not a redesign. Prioritized below.

---

## P0 — Ship before driving any traffic (cheap, high-leverage, or reputation-critical)

1. **Surface the free scan tool in/near the hero.** Currently the scan tool — our single highest-converting element and primary cold-outreach hook — lives well below the fold in its own section. Add a lightweight "enter your URL" input in or immediately under the homepage hero so the hook and the action share one eyeful. Every scroll before the CTA leaks conversions. Keep the full scan section too; this is just an earlier entry point.

2. **Verify the two cited stats resolve to real primary sources.** The blog post cites "75% of people judge credibility on website design" (should link to the Stanford Web Credibility research) and "53% of mobile visitors leave after 3 seconds" (should link to Google's mobile page-speed research). Make sure both links point to actual primary sources, not aggregators. Most SMB owners won't check — the one who does will judge us on it.

---

## P1 — Conversion improvements

3. **Convert the before/after proof section from "portfolio" to "results."** Current thumbnails are small and the actual improvement isn't legible at a glance. Add a concrete metric per case study, e.g. "load time 6.2s → 1.1s", "Lighthouse 48 → 100", "+40% mobile conversions". "Here's a result I get" is a far stronger claim than "here's a site I made." Make the numbers the visual focal point of each card.

4. **Add 1–2 named testimonials with a business name (and face if possible).** For cold SMB traffic landing from a PageSpeed scan, trust signals are thin. The About bio builds trust in *me*; a client voice builds trust in *the outcome*. Place near the proof section or the pricing block.

5. **Reframe the About intro to keep OCT credibility without the "side project" read.** Currently names current employer (Ontario College of Teachers) as a day job, which some clients read as "moonlighting — will he still be here in a year / will he prioritize me." Reframe toward experience rather than current employment (e.g. "I build accessible web tools professionally / 4+ years frontend dev experience") — keep the credibility, drop the moonlighting signal. Keep the honest solo-founder tone; specificity is what makes the page believable. (Judgment call — some clients find the transparency reassuring, so keep it warm and personal.)

6. **Trim the contact form to reduce friction.** Name + email + message is enough to start a conversation; every optional field costs completions. Keep "Your current website (optional)" — but make it do double duty: if a URL is provided, trigger/queue a scan so the first reply can open with something concrete about their site.

---

## P1 — Traffic / SEO (the blog is the engine, it's already built right)

7. **Loosen blog-card excerpt truncation.** Excerpts cut off too early ("Here's what…", "it's losing you real customers. Here's why Wix…"). One more visible line per card improves click-through without hurting layout.

8. **Keep and lean into the internal-linking web in blog posts.** The cross-linking between the credibility post, "why Wix is slow," redesign-cost post, and the free-audit CTA is already excellent and compounds for SEO. Maintain this pattern on every new post: each post should link 2–4 related posts + route to the free audit at the end.

9. **Keep the "when it's NOT worth it" honesty sections.** The self-disqualifying content ("when a custom site is not worth it") reads as credible and converts better than a hard sell. Pattern to repeat.

---

## P2 — Polish (do when convenient)

10. **Open up blog-post body line-height slightly.** Measure/width is fine but line-height is a touch tight for a long read.

---

## What NOT to change

- Don't redesign. The visual system (cream/mint/near-black, green accent, rounded cards, type hierarchy) is coherent and carries cleanly across all pages — that's the hard part and it's done.
- Don't bury the scan tool — it's our best acquisition hook.
- Don't touch the overall funnel narrative on the homepage (hook → problem agitation → free scan → what changes after launch → proof → personal/trust → transparent pricing → FAQ → final CTA). It's a textbook problem-aware→solution flow and it's working.
- Don't remove the two-path pricing ("no surprise invoices" framing directly answers the biggest SMB objection).
